An overview of how Wayfarers plays, with links to the detailed rules.

- **Tiers instead of proficiency.** Many bonuses scale by [[Tiers|tier level]] (1–4) rather than a proficiency bonus.
- **Attributes 1–10.** Six [[Attributes]], starting from an [[Starting Attribute Arrays|array]] around 12 points.
- **Action Points.** Turns run on a pool of [[Action Points]] instead of action/bonus action.
- **Boosting.** Spend attribute points to boost a roll ([[Boosting and Pushing]]).
- **Scenes.** Many abilities refresh per [[Scenes|scene]].
- **Skill challenges** use named difficulty ranks ([[Skill Challenges]]).
- **Statuses matter.** Damage types apply [[Conditions and Statuses|statuses]], and vulnerabilities are a big part of combat ([[Damage Types]]).
- **Cooldown spellcasting.** No spell slots ([[Spellcasting]]).
- **15-second rounds**, with gridless ranges: close (within 30 ft), near (30–60 ft), far (60 ft+).

> [!question] What die do skill checks use?
> The design notes list "Rolling d6 for skill checks" and "Stunts: rolling the same dice number on a skill check", which suggests multiple d6s. But the difficulty ranks run from 5 to 50, and many rules mention d20-style advantage, crit ranges of 19–20, and saving throws. The core resolution roll needs to be written down.

> [!question] Defense Value, AC, or saves?
> Most rules use **DV** (Defense Value) and "tests" with a TV (target value), but [[Cover and Obscurement]] still uses AC and Dexterity saving throws, and some abilities say "saving throw", "save" or "DC". The notes also ask for **two defense systems**: one against damage and one against status effects (see [[Defense]]).
