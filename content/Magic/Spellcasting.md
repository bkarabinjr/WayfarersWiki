## Cooldowns instead of spell slots

Spells don't use spell slots; they have **cooldowns**. So characters have fewer spells equipped at the ready. Full casters in 5e have up to 8 spells by level 5 and cap around 15–20. In Wayfarers, 20 spells is far too many and would make cooldowns trivial. The goal is to cut the number of spells a player can use **by at least half**.

Spells are streamlined, so there's less spell bloat. Many spells, even cantrips, have multiple uses and can be **upcast**.

## Initial numbers

- **Cantrips** are tied to your spell attribute.
- **Full casters** get two spells per tier level.
- **Half casters** get one spell per tier level.

## Spell tiers

Higher-tier spells are more potent than lower-tier ones, but lower-tier spells stay viable. Not every spell needs an upcast. *Like Pokémon: not every Pokémon needs a full evolution line; some have only one or two stages.* The more variety in where spells sit across tiers, the better.

**Target: under 300 spells total.** Fewer spells, each with more options, should add importance and texture to spellcasting.

## Schools, tags and attributes

- Spell schools are reworked to classify spells better.
- Spell descriptors are reworked into a **spell tag** system (on the [[Roadmap]]).
- **Every spell has an attribute.** Critical hits with spells give extra options based on that attribute.

## Spell timing

Certain spells get a bonus depending on *when* in the round they're cast relative to other creatures. Lightning spells might have an extra effect against creatures that haven't acted yet; earth spells against those that have. This gives reasons to use different spells at different times.

## Keeping it in check

Because spells have cooldowns, casters can cast multiple spells per turn, and spells are more versatile, so spells will seem much more efficient. To compensate, the design leans toward reducing some aspects of spells: damage, range, or duration.

## Spell lists

From the Additional Info notes, each tradition has a role:

| Tradition | Role |
|---|---|
| **Arcane** | Single-target to area spells, no healing |
| **Primal** | Single- or multi-target damage, area control |
| **Divine** | Single-target to area healing |
| **Psionic** | *Not decided* |

The same notes group magic into **Elemental** (fire, frost…), **Mystical** and **Carnal** types. *Carnal* isn't defined yet.
