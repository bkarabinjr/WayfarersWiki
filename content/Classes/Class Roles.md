Every class is fully capable on its own, and you can customize your character however you want, but classes fall into **roles** they generally excel in.

The source also lists these building blocks for roles: *weaponry, protection, support, divine, arcane, primal, psionic, movement, social, tactics, utility.*

## Vanguard

The warrior classes, masters of melee combat. They generally have the best protection and excellent close-up offense, and are the primary front-line combatants. They have abilities that help allies maneuver around the battlefield and make things difficult for enemies. **Vanguard classes get attacks of opportunity.**

[[Berserker]] · [[Soldier]] · [[Martial Artist]] · [[Guardian]]

## Paragon

The mage classes, the most proficient in their magical origin. Potent spellcasting: they can affect large areas and deal with groups of enemies at once through crowd control, hazardous terrain, or hampering foes. **Full spell list.**

[[Arcanist]] · [[Cleric]] · [[Druid]] · [[Psion]]

## Specialist

Experts in certain tasks or skills, usually favoring cunning over brawn. Versatile, skill-based classes, normally with high utility and single-target damage. **Bonus skill points.**

[[Artisan]] · [[Sage]] · [[Ranger]] · [[Rogue]]

## Gifted

Versatile support classes: status effects and social situations. They inspire and assist other characters. They have good offense and defense, but their strength is in powers that protect allies and reveal enemies' vulnerabilities.

[[Bard]] · [[Duskblade]] · [[Sorcerer]] · [[Warlock]]

> [!question] Role names
> One version of this section calls the roles "the warrior, the mage, the expert, and the gifted"; the other calls them Vanguard, Paragon, Specialist and Gifted. This page uses the second set.

> [!note] Each class needs
> Every class entry in the source ends with "In exploration []. In social situations []": each class still needs its exploration and social hooks (see [[Design Pillars]]).
